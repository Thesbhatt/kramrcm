The one rule that matters: the logo orange on white is only 2.67:1. That fails WCAG for text and for white-text buttons. So:
  • Orange buttons use dark text, not white (6.2:1).
  • Orange text on light backgrounds uses Orange Ink #A9490C.
  • Bright orange text is fine on Graphite Deep (5.6:1).

Why this pairing

  • Source Serif 4 (Adobe, open source) has optical sizes, so big headlines look refined and small ones stay sturdy. It rhymes with the serif "KRAM" wordmark.
  • Public Sans was made for the U.S. Web Design System. It's tuned for long reading and forms and has tabular numbers for stats.
  • Neither face is used by the three references, so Kram won't look like a sibling of Knack (Fraunces/Poppins) or Credsage (Instrument Serif/Manrope).
  • Both are free on Google Fonts and can be self-hosted with next/font for speed and privacy.

Get in touch form

Short, safe, and sent through Resend

Seven fields, four required. Knack asks for nine required fields; that costs leads. We ask only what sales needs for the first call.

  1. Form validates in the browser (React Hook Form + Zod).
  2. A Next.js server action re-validates, checks a honeypot and Cloudflare Turnstile, and rate-limits by IP.
  3. Resend sends the lead to sales@kramhealthcare.com with a reply-to of the visitor.
  4. Resend sends the visitor a branded confirmation ("We'll reply within one business day").
  5. The page shows an inline success message. No third-party ad pixels fire on this page.

Domain must be verified in Resend (SPF, DKIM, DMARC on kramhealthcare.com) so emails don't land in spam.

US healthcare checklist

What must be there, and what must not

Must have do

  • WCAG 2.1 AA accessibility. HHS's Section 504 rule makes it the standard for HHS-funded providers (deadline moved to May 11, 2027). Kram's clients will expect vendors to meet it, and it protects against ADA demand letters.
  • Real proof. Stats with a footnote, named testimonials, client logos with permission.
  • Compliance stated precisely: "HIPAA-compliant processes" and "We sign a BAA". Show SOC 2 or similar only if Kram actually holds it.
  • A physical US address, real phone in header and footer, business hours with time zone.
  • Privacy Policy, Terms, Accessibility statement linked in the footer.
  • PHI warning on every form and clear consent wording.
  • US spelling and terms (Orthopedics, payer, practice manager) and US date/phone formats.
  • Fast pages: Core Web Vitals green, images in AVIF/WebP, fonts self-hosted.
  • SEO: one page per service and specialty, unique titles, schema.org Organization + FAQ markup.

Avoid don't

  • "HIPAA certified". There is no such certification; compliance buyers notice.
  • Meta Pixel or ad trackers on forms. A Texas court vacated part of HHS's tracking guidance, but private class actions over pixels continue. Use privacy-friendly analytics.
  • Fake or unsourced numbers, stock testimonials, or AI-generated doctor photos.
  • Hero carousels, autoplay video with sound, pop-ups on load, chatbots that invite health details.
  • Long required forms and many CTA labels for one action. One label: "Get in touch" / "Book a free practice review".
  • Content only JavaScript can render (Credsage) or stats that show "0" without it (Knack).
  • Pre-checked SMS opt-in. Texting needs separate, explicit consent under the TCPA.
  • Low-contrast orange text on white.
Before design starts

Questions for the client

  1. Can we keep the four stats (10+, 5K+, 120K+, 99%)? How is each measured, and as of what date?
  2. Which compliance claims are true today: BAA signed with clients? SOC 2, ISO 27001, or HITRUST? Any AAPC-certified coders?
  3. Can we get 2–3 testimonials with name, title, practice and state, and permission to show any client logos?
  4. What US office address and business hours should appear? Is the team US-based, offshore, or mixed?
  5. Is the logo available as SVG? Are they open to a light logo refresh (cleaner mark for small sizes)?
  6. Which 8 specialties matter most for sales? These get detail pages first.
  7. Who receives form leads, and should they also go to a CRM (HubSpot, Zoho)?
  8. Do they want pricing shown ("starting at" or percentage of collections), or "custom quote" only?
